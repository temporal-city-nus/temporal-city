Information · the city, after hours

The City, After Hours.

Exploring spatial and temporal urban availability in Singapore. Mixed-use planning records which land uses sit together; this project adds when their amenities are available, builds the opening-hours data needed to measure it, and compares the two.

Research questionHow might different spatial land-use compositions be associated with different patterns of amenity availability across the day, and how can this temporal dimension support the comparison of mixed-use planning scenarios?

01 · Research background & problem

Does a mixed-use city remain accessible throughout the day?

Mixed in space is not mixed in time.

Research background

Mix describes where, not when

Land-use mix describes which functions coexist, but not when their amenities are available.

Research aim

Relate composition to 24 hours

Examine the relationship between land-use composition and 24-hour amenity availability in three Singapore neighbourhoods.

Planning relevance

Find the gaps

Identify periods of limited amenity availability and provide evidence for comparing mixed-use areas.

Same neighbourhood. Different availability.

Day · 12:00

Most facilities open

Food, retail, school, services, culture and civic facilities are all open.

Night · 23:00

Few facilities open

Mainly food, retail and services remain open; schools, civic and most culture facilities close.

Conceptual illustration. In the Google Places sample, Food & Beverage and Retail make up nearly two-thirds of the amenities open between 22:00 and 05:00 on weekdays.

The idea is well established (Jacobs, 1961; Montgomery, 1998; Hoppenbrouwer & Louw, 2005), and opening hours are known to shape access (Delafontaine et al., 2011; Gehrke & Clifton, 2016). What is missing is a planning-oriented framework that links land-use composition to 24-hour amenity availability and uses that link to compare mixed-use scenarios.

Scope: the project measures scheduled availability. Footfall, occupancy and urban vitality are not outputs.

02 · Project logic

From the problem to the planning decision.

Land-use mix (X) and amenity availability (Y) are measured from separate sources, then related hour by hour to inform the land-use mix of a site or precinct.

X · Land-use mix: 6 Master Plan classes · entropy H (0–1) · GFA-weighted H→Y · Availability: 24 h × 6 categories · POIs + opening hours · relative + absolute

Separate sources → Y is never predicted from itself.

Five steps from data to the planning decision

1 · Collect

Collector

Amenities and opening hours for any station area; AI reads hours from website text.

2 · Verify

Review

Quality labels and evidence for every value; sample audit still to run.

3 · Relate

Atlas

Hourly maps, GFA layer, compare up to 8 areas.

4 · Apply

Planning uses

Diagnose gaps, benchmark, write briefs, check who is served.

5 · Decide

Land-use mix

URA planners and developers' design teams set the land-use proportions.

Now

Describe and compare existing areas

GIS and the Atlas relate X and Y: hourly availability per area and category, weekday vs weekend, GFA-weighted availability. Compare an area with reference areas and re-run to monitor change.

Future

Compare candidate mixes

A regression baseline, then tree-based models (Random Forest, Gradient Boosting) tested on held-out catchments. A planner enters 2–3 candidate mixes and compares their predicted 24-hour profiles.

The tool informs the comparison; the decision stays with the planner, weighed with accessibility, feasibility and policy goals. Beyond asking what land uses coexist here, planners can ask what pattern of amenity availability a composition produces across the day.

Why the opening-hours data changed

The mid-term OSM inventory had too few opening hours for a 24-hour analysis, so the group connected the Google Places API.

Before · mid-term · OpenStreetMap (dropped)
18.6%Only 159 of 853 relevant POIs carry an opening-hours tag; none in Education or Community & Civic. Too sparse to build Y.
Now · Google Places API (used)
78.3%646 records returned; 595 fall inside the official 400 m catchments (51 outside excluded); 466 of these have weekly hours, 129 remain Unknown.

These 595 records replace the OSM inventory as the project's sample, and all results use them.

The two percentages describe different inventories, so they show why the source changed rather than a like-for-like improvement.

Amenity categories

Places are sorted into six functional categories — Food & Beverage, Retail, Services, Education, Community & Civic, Culture & Recreation. Non-functional objects such as benches, parking, toilets and ATMs are removed. In the Collector, POIs from different OSM tags (amenity, shop, tourism, leisure, craft, healthcare) are sorted into the same six categories.

05 · Method

How X and Y are measured.

The project relates two measures: X, land-use mix from the Master Plan, and Y, amenity availability from opening hours. Each is built separately, then read together.

X · Land-use mix→Y · Amenity availability
X

Land-use mix

From the URA Master Plan

H = − Σ pᵢ ln pᵢ ÷ ln 6 (0 = one class, 1 = an even mix of six)

Entropy uses the land area of six planning classes (Residential, Mixed, Commercial, Hotel, Civic & Education, Open space); roads, water and transport are excluded.

GFA(parcel) = clipped parcel area × numeric GPR

Non-numeric GPR codes (LND, EVA, SDP) add no GFA, so GFA measures are partial and their coverage is reported. GFA-weighted availability weights each parcel's open share by its GFA: a parcel-level proxy, not floor area physically open.

Y

Amenity availability

06 · Future AI

Predict and compare

From land-use mix to 24-hour profiles.

How AI is used

Step 03 · in use AI

Read the hours

Websites write hours as sentences or tables (“Mon–Fri 9am to 6pm; closed at weekends”) that rules cannot always read. The model gets the passage with the place's name and address, picks the right branch and returns a 7-day, 24-hour schedule as JSON, quoting the sentence it used. It runs only where rules fail.

Step 06 · future AI

Predict and compare

A model would learn how land-use mix relates to 24-hour availability across many catchments, checked against a regression baseline, so planners can compare 2–3 candidate mixes. Three areas are too few to train it; the data must first be complete and validated.

  • Checked quoteThe quoted sentence must be in the page, or the result is rejected.
  • Ranked last among sourcesBelow Google Places and OSM, above rule parsing; missing hours get a guided Google check by hand.
  • RepeatableTemperature 0; any model can be used (Ollama, Gemini, Groq, DeepSeek, OpenAI, Claude).
  • Only trusted labels analysedverified, observed and retrieved hours; conflicting and estimated hours are left out.

Y2 · From opening hours to hourly states

Each schedule becomes a 7 × 24 grid of open, closed or unknown. An hour counts as open if the place is open for any part of it (11:30 fills the 11:00 slot), so half-hour openings are kept; past-midnight hours carry into the next day.

Y3 · Availability

Relative availability(h)=open POIs at h ÷ POIs with usable opening hoursAbsolute availability(h)=number of POIs open at h

Reported by area and category for weekdays (Monday–Friday average), Saturday and Sunday. Places without hours are left out of the denominator, never counted as closed. Functional diversity applies the entropy formula to the six categories open each hour. Summary windows: daytime 09:00–18:00, late night 22:00–05:00.

06 · Limitations

What to read with care.

Each limit, why it matters, and how we mitigate it.

AThe AI approach

Limit

Why it matters

Mitigation

Misreading

The model can pick the wrong branch or miss past-midnight and holiday rules. The quote check proves the sentence exists, not that it was read correctly.

Ranked below Google and OSM; cross-checked; conflicts reviewed by hand.

Accuracy not yet measured

The sample relies mostly on Google, so the AI path is untested at scale, and its confidence is a fixed score, not measured accuracy.

Validate accuracy per method; calibrate confidence on that sample.

What it can read

Hours in images, PDF menus or JavaScript pages are missed; third-party pages can be outdated; some sites forbid scraping.

Dated evidence links; social and booking sites skipped; Google check by hand.

Future model not built

Three catchments cannot train or test a model that predicts 24-hour profiles from land-use mix.

Multi-site sample and a regression baseline first.

BData, method and scope

Limit

Why it matters

Mitigation

Coverage and accuracy

Hawkers and small operators are under-recorded, and having hours does not make them right; no independent audit yet.

Guided Google check; site visits; stratified audit (95% Wilson interval) still to run.

Scheduled, not actual use

Opening hours only: holidays and temporary closures are not modelled, and open does not mean busy.

Dated records with refresh; read with footfall data.

Land use and GFA

Land use records the main planned use, not vertical mix; GFA uses numeric GPR only (planned capacity); case areas use MP2019, new areas MP2025.

Treated as proxies; coverage and source stated in every data note.

Google data terms

The case-area sample comes from the Google Places API and is shown for non-commercial coursework only.

Source credited (“Places data © Google”); not for reuse; other areas use each user’s own key.

Scope and interpretation

Three Central Area cases show an association, not a cause; night activity helps some users and burdens others.

Multi-site sample; results reported by category.
